I spent over a decade as a professional firefighter and paramedic in Oregon, and served as a 68W combat medic in the Army National Guard. I've been through recruit academy and probation. I've worked the rotations, watched how overtime gets sold, and seen how quickly “we're a family” can turn out to be a slogan.

I know what it takes to get into this career — the testing, the agility exams, the psych evaluations, the backgrounds, the interviews, the ride-alongs — and what it takes to keep showing up year after year. I also know what it's like to be a woman in this field, in a workforce that wasn't built with both genders in mind.

That's the part I can't teach another therapist. You won't spend the first six sessions explaining your schedule, your chain of command, or why you can't just call in.

How I work

The first session is longer, and mostly listening.

I want a real history — a conversation, not a form — so we can tell early whether I'm the right fit and so I understand what's actually going on around you. The deep work comes later and it needs a foundation.

With couples, I'll meet with each of you individually within the first few sessions. Two people rarely describe the same relationship the same way, and I want both versions.

Weekly is the standard rhythm — things need time to settle in between. Most people work with me over a period of years rather than weeks.

What I draw on

  • Cognitive Behavioral Therapy
  • CBT-I for insomnia
  • Structural family therapy
  • Gottman method for couples
  • Solution-focused work
  • Experiential approaches

No role plays.

Fit

Where I'm not the right therapist.

Being clear about this protects you. I see one appointment per client per week, which means I'm not the right fit for anyone in active crisis or needing a higher level of care.

I also don't provide gender-affirming care, sex therapy, or addiction-focused treatment. If you reach out and I'm not the right person, I'll tell you on the consultation call and point you somewhere better rather than take you on.
